Trackypady

Privacy.

Effective November 23, 2026. Trackypady and Trackypady Bridge are developed by Deep Interaction Lab Inc. This policy covers our iPhone controller, Mac companion and setup website.

On your devices

Trackypady sends gesture commands to the Mac you pair with and receives connection, action and haptic status. The current connection uses Bluetooth with encrypted, authenticated messages. The apps store pairing credentials in the device Keychain and preferences locally. Device names and installation identifiers help recognize the paired devices; they are not advertising identifiers.

The iPhone camera is used to scan the Mac’s pairing QR. Trackypady does not upload camera images or record microphone audio on your iPhone. The gesture demo uses sample text and does not send commands to Codex.

Codex and Mac permissions

With your permission, Bridge uses macOS Accessibility to inspect and operate Codex controls, including its composer and selection. It reads relevant local Codex settings and application/log information to resolve shortcuts, recording state and compatibility. Configuring shortcuts can update Codex’s local keybindings file and save a backup.

Dictation is performed by Codex using the microphone selected on your Mac. Prompts, recordings and responses handled by Codex are subject to that service’s terms and privacy practices. Trackypady is a separate companion; Deep Interaction Lab does not operate Codex.

Servers, diagnostics and support

Trackypady has no DIL account system, advertising SDK or analytics uploader. We do not operate a server that receives your gesture session, pairing credentials, dictated audio or Codex draft text. Diagnostic events such as action types, timestamps and errors may appear in local app or system logs. Bridge shows a bounded recent activity list. Trackypady itself does not upload diagnostics to us.

When you install Trackypady through TestFlight, Apple automatically collects crash and usage information and shares it with Deep Interaction Lab. If you send feedback through TestFlight, we receive that too. We use this information to fix problems and improve Trackypady.

If you get in touch, we receive the information you choose to send, such as your email, device/build details and screenshots. We use it to answer your questions and look into any issues you raise. Do not send pairing QR codes, passwords or private conversations. We retain correspondence while needed to respond and follow up; contact us to request its deletion, subject to applicable recordkeeping obligations.

Website and external links

Our static setup pages and Mac downloads are hosted by GitHub. GitHub and Apple may process ordinary request information when you visit their pages or download software, under their own privacy policies. Our pages use no analytics scripts, advertising trackers or third-party embedded resources. A pairing link carries its credential in the URL fragment, which browsers do not send in the website request. The pairing page removes that fragment from browser history and does not append it to Apple download links. Treat the QR and full pairing link as private.

Your choices

You can revoke Camera or Bluetooth permission in iPhone Settings and Accessibility permission in Mac System Settings. Forgetting a pairing removes its active control access. The apps may retain a local pending revocation record until the other device can be notified. Forgetting does not delete Codex chats, recordings, operating-system logs or correspondence with us. Keep both apps updated and forget devices you no longer use.

Contact and changes

For questions about Trackypady or privacy, get in touch at info@deepinteractionlab.com. We will update this page and its effective date when these practices change.
